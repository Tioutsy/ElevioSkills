## 1. Overview of Pedagogical Standards Applied

Each course in Batch 1 has been restructured according to the 10-point workplace learning architecture:
1. **Hook**: Tangible workplace dilemma.
2. **Why it matters**: Operational, economic, and environmental impact.
3. **Core understanding**: Plain-language concept explanations.
4. **Mauritian & Sector Context**: Local operational realities.
5. **Practical actions**: Procedural controls and checklists.
6. **Decision Scenarios**: $\ge 2$ interactive scenarios with branching consequence feedback.
7. **Assessment**: $\ge 8$ scored items with complete option feedback on all distractors.
8. **Workplace Action**: 1 achievable 30-day operational commitment.
9. **Completion**: Restrained professional recognition.
10. **Next Recommendation**: Pathway-aligned progression.

## 2. Course-by-Course Instructional Evidence

### Course 1: `ELH-01` — Sustainability Foundations & ESG Core Principles
- **Evidenced Level**: **D1 (Foundational Awareness)**
- **Level Justification**: Focuses on recognizing the interconnectedness of Environmental, Social, and Governance pillars, distinguishing awareness from evidence, and identifying everyday administrative/operational waste reduction actions without requiring complex statistical emissions modeling (which belongs in D3/D4).
- **Primary Competency**: `COMP_SUSTAINABILITY_FOUNDATIONS`
- **Learning Objectives**:
  1. Explain the interconnected ESG pillars in simple commercial language.
  2. Distinguish passive awareness from verifiable operational ESG evidence.
  3. Identify high-impact everyday decisions across employee roles.
  4. Evaluate trade-offs between short-term convenience and sustainability.

#### Decision Scenarios:
1. **Scenario 1 (Procurement Trade-off)**: Evaluating Supplier Environmental Claims (Unverified marketing vs certified local take-back aluminum).
   - *Best Response*: Select certified take-back vendor.
   - *Pedagogical Feedback*: Reinforces third-party verification and closed-loop disposal over greenwashing.
2. **Scenario 2 (Social/Safety Dilemma)**: Contractor Safety Under Project Deadlines (Working without PPE near high voltage).
   - *Best Response*: Halt electrical work immediately until safety protocols are met.
   - *Pedagogical Feedback*: Establishes that duty of care and human life strictly supersede commercial schedule pressures.

#### Assessment Set (8 Items):
- **Item 1** (Evidence Type: UNDERSTANDING | Difficulty: D1): Definition of operational sustainability.
  - *Correct*: Meeting needs without compromising future capacity.
  - *Feedback*: Covers all options with explanations of why philanthropy or arbitrary bans are insufficient.
- **Item 2** (Evidence Type: UNDERSTANDING | Difficulty: D1): Identification of the three ESG pillars.
- **Item 3** (Evidence Type: APPLICATION | Difficulty: D1): Why carbon reduction cannot offset labor abuses.
- **Item 4** (Evidence Type: APPLICATION | Difficulty: D1): Distinguishing verifiable evidence from greenwashing.
- **Item 5** (Evidence Type: APPLICATION | Difficulty: D1): Zero-cost office energy and paper reduction behaviors.
- **Item 6** (Evidence Type: SCENARIO / JUDGEMENT | Difficulty: D1): Responding to executive pressure to conceal chemical spills.
- **Item 7** (Evidence Type: APPLICATION | Difficulty: D1): Total Cost of Ownership life-cycle reviews.
- **Item 8** (Evidence Type: SCENARIO / JUDGEMENT | Difficulty: D1): Standard for communicating sustainability claims to stakeholders.

### Course 2: `ELH-02` — Climate Change Science & Carbon Accounting Essentials
- **Evidenced Level**: **D1 (Foundational Awareness)**
- **Level Justification**: Explains the greenhouse effect mechanism, definitions of Scope 1, Scope 2, and Scope 3, and raw activity data (liters of fuel, kWh of electricity) versus calculated emissions. Does not require multi-variable regression modeling or regulatory CSRD taxonomy calculations (D3/D4).
- **Primary Competency**: `COMP_GHG`
- **Learning Objectives**:
  1. Explain the greenhouse gas mechanism and CO2e metric.
  2. Categorize emissions accurately into Scope 1, Scope 2, and Scope 3.
  3. Differentiate activity data from calculated emission metrics.
  4. Identify operational decisions that reduce electricity and fuel burn.

#### Decision Scenarios:
1. **Scenario 1 (Power Management)**: Running backup diesel generator vs peak grid tariff and load management.
   - *Best Response*: Load management over generator running to avoid massive Scope 1 emissions.
2. **Scenario 2 (Logistics Dispatch)**: Ad-hoc van dispatch vs zone-based route consolidation.
   - *Best Response*: Zone route scheduling cutting distance by 30%.

#### Assessment Set (8 Items):
- **Item 1** (UNDERSTANDING | D1): Standardized metric `tCO2e`.
- **Item 2** (APPLICATION | D1): Identifying Scope 1 direct sources (diesel generator / company vans).
- **Item 3** (UNDERSTANDING | D1): Mechanism of Scope 2 purchased grid electricity emissions.
- **Item 4** (UNDERSTANDING | D1): Formula connecting activity data to calculated emissions.
- **Item 5** (APPLICATION | D1): Air conditioning setpoint optimization (24°C vs 19°C).
- **Item 6** (SCENARIO / JUDGEMENT | D1): Defect in claiming carbon neutrality by planting unverified seedlings against heavy diesel burn.
- **Item 7** (APPLICATION | D1): Identifying Scope 3 freight and supply chain levers.
- **Item 8** (APPLICATION | D1): Vehicle maintenance and tire pressure influence on fuel economy.
